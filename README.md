Food & Beverage (Quick Service - Retail)
2.5 Story of the Data
This dataset captures customer purchasing behavior and sales performance in a pizza retail business across different time periods, product types, and customer preferences.
It helps the business understand what sells, when it sells, how much is earned, and which products and sizes drive revenue, enabling data-driven decisions on pricing, promotions, inventory, and staffing.

3. In Analysis
3.1 Units sold by weekday
Observations
1.	Sunday records the highest quantity sold (7,396 units), making it the strongest sales day.
2.	Wednesday has the lowest sales volume (6,900 units) among all weekdays.
3.	Weekend days (Saturday and Sunday) outperform most weekdays in total quantity sold.
4.	Friday sales (7,108 units) show increased demand leading into the weekend.
5.	Sales volumes across weekdays are relatively balanced, ranging between 6,900 and 7,396 units.
Pre-Insights
1.	Weekends drive the highest customer demand, especially Sundays.
2.	Midweek periods, particularly Tuesday and Wednesday, may benefit from targeted promotions.
3.	Staffing and inventory should be scaled up on weekends and Fridays.
4.	Sales stability suggests the business can reliably forecast demand.
5.	Sunday demand may be driven by family and group consumption.
3.2 Quantity Sold by Pizza Size
Observations
1.	Medium-sized pizzas record the highest quantity sold (15,635 units).
2.	Small pizzas (14,403 units) closely follow medium sizes in demand.
3.	Extra Large pizzas (14,371 units) show similarly strong performance.
4.	There is a significant drop in sales volume for Large pizzas (5,165 units).
5.	Customer purchasing patterns favor practical portion sizes over traditional large options.
6.	Medium, Small, and Extra Large sizes collectively account for the vast majority of total sales.
Pre-Insights
1.	Medium pizzas are the most popular choice, likely offering the best value-for-money perception.
2.	Traditional Large pizzas may be priced unattractively or perceived as less valuable.
3.	There may be an opportunity to reposition or reprice Large pizzas.
4.	Bundles built around Medium and Extra Large sizes could increase average order value.
5.	Inventory should prioritize Medium and Extra Large size ingredients.
3.3 Monthly Sales Volume
Observations
1.	January records the highest sales volume (4,369 units) across all months.
2.	February has the lowest quantity sold (3,771 units).
3.	Sales volume increases steadily from February to June, indicating demand recovery.
4.	June is one of the strongest months (4,311 units), closely following January.
5.	A noticeable dip occurs in July (3,975 units) after the mid-year peak.
6.	Sales volumes stabilize between August and December, averaging slightly above 4,100 units.
7.	December sales (4,211 units) remain strong, suggesting sustained end-of-year demand.
Pre-Insights
1.	Post-holiday demand appears strong in January, likely influenced by promotions or increased dining out.
2.	February’s low sales may reflect shorter days or seasonal consumer behavior.
3.	The rise from March to June suggests increasing consumer activity during the first half of the year.
4.	Mid-year demand softens slightly after June, possibly due to vacations or seasonal shifts.
5.	Stable sales from August to December indicate a resilient customer base.
6.	December performance suggests pizza consumption remains popular during festive periods.
3.4 Top-Selling Products by Quantity
Observations
1.	Vegetables Pizza is the top-selling product, recording 5,341 units sold.
2.	Chicken Pizza (3,820 units) and Cheese Pizza (3,801 units) follow closely behind.
3.	There is a significant gap between Vegetables Pizza and the second-best seller.
4.	Deluxe Pizza (2,453 units) and Barbecue Pizza (2,432 units) show similar performance levels.
5.	Hawaiian Pizza (2,422 units) records the lowest quantity among the top products.
6.	All listed products maintain meaningful sales volumes, indicating menu balance.
Pre-Insights
1.	Vegetables Pizza has broad appeal, possibly driven by health-conscious and vegetarian customers.
2.	Chicken and Cheese pizzas show consistent and stable demand, not reliant on seasonality.
3.	High-volume products present opportunities for bundles and promotional deals.
4.	Top-selling items should be prioritized for inventory planning and stock optimization.
5.	Menu optimization efforts should focus on high-volume, high-margin pizzas
3.5 Revenue by Pizza Product
Observations
1.	Vegetables Pizza generates the highest revenue at $89,148.50.
2.	Cheese Pizza ($69,920.70) and Chicken Pizza ($66,741.50) closely follow in revenue contribution.
3.	Barbecue Pizza ($42,768.00) and California Pizza ($41,409.50) generate similar revenue levels.
4.	Revenue drops gradually from the top-selling product to the lowest performer.
5.	The top three pizzas account for a significant portion of total product revenue.
6.	Total revenue from these products amounts to $309,988.20.
Pre-Insights
1.	Vegetables Pizza is both a volume and value driver.
2.	Cheese and Chicken pizzas reflect consistent customer preference and strong repeat purchases.
3.	Mid-tier products like Barbecue and California pizzas may be priced competitively but sell at moderate volume.
4.	Classic flavor profiles outperform specialty or novelty options.
5.	Product pricing appears aligned with customer demand.
6.	Menu optimization should protect top earners while testing improvements for mid-tier products.
3.6 Revenue by Pizza Category
Observations
1.	Classic pizzas generate the highest revenue, totaling $446,089.90.
2.	Classic category alone contributes more than half of total revenue.
3.	Chicken pizzas are the second-highest revenue contributor at $172,379.50.
4.	Veggie pizzas generate $97,063.80, showing moderate revenue performance.
5.	Supreme pizzas contribute $61,791.80 to total revenue.
6.	Custom pizzas generate the lowest revenue at $40,535.05.
Pre-Insights
1.	Classic pizzas are the backbone of business revenue, driving financial stability.
2.	Strong Chicken pizza revenue indicates consistent customer preference for protein-based options.
3.	Veggie pizzas perform well but may benefit from premium pricing or positioning.
4.	Supreme pizzas may appeal to niche or premium customers rather than volume buyers.
5.	Low Custom pizza revenue suggests limited adoption or operational inefficiency.
6.	Pricing strategy heavily favors standardized menu items over customization.
7.	Marketing investment should prioritize Classic and Chicken categories.
4. Post Analysis
4.1	General Observations
1.	Pizza sales show strong consistency across weekdays, with weekends—especially Sundays—driving the highest volumes.
2.	Monthly sales volumes remain stable throughout the year, with peaks in January and June and minimal seasonal volatility.
3.	Medium-sized pizzas are the most popular, followed closely by Small and Extra Large sizes.
4.	Large pizzas significantly underperform, indicating a shift in size preference.
5.	Vegetables Pizza leads in both quantity sold and individual product revenue.
6.	Classic pizza category dominates total revenue, contributing over half of overall sales value.
7.	Chicken and Cheese pizzas show consistent demand, supporting revenue stability.
8.	Sales performance is driven more by volume than price, highlighting strong customer demand.
9.	Revenue is concentrated among a few high-performing categories and products, following the Pareto principle.
10.	Overall demand patterns suggest a reliable and predictable sales cycle, enabling effective forecasting.
4.2	General Recommendations
1.	Prioritize weekend staffing, inventory, and delivery capacity, especially on Sundays.
2.	Introduce midweek promotions (Tuesday–Wednesday) to lift lower-demand days.
3.	Anchor marketing campaigns around Medium- and Extra-Large pizzas, which drive volume.
4.	Reevaluate the Large pizza size, consider repricing, resizing, or repositioning.
5.	Protect and promote Vegetables, Cheese, and Chicken pizzas as flagship products.
6.	Use Classic category pizzas as bundle anchors to boost average order value.
7.	Test targeted seasonal promotions around slower months like February.
8.	Simplify or optimize low-performing menu items to reduce operational complexity.
9.	Leverage sales consistency to improve forecasting, inventory planning, and cost control.
10.	Invest in data-driven menu engineering to balance high-volume and high-margin products.
